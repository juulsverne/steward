# Steward after the competition

This preserves company direction. It is not a weekend backlog.

The eventual product is an autonomous operating layer for physical communities. A district supplies a geographic area, policy, budget, approved providers, and authority. Steward maintains that area through existing systems and surfaces decisions outside its authority or confidence.

Signals may eventually come from residents, opt-in community channels, government systems, operators, crews, and authorized cameras/IoT/fleet sensors. They resolve into canonical physical issues, then evidence, jurisdiction, plans, providers, verification, and auditable spending. A resident can dispute an administratively completed issue. **Job closed does not equal problem resolved.**

The initial buyer is an SSA, BID, campus, property district, or mixed-use development with an existing supplemental maintenance budget and provider network. Steward complements baseline city services by improving speed, certainty, verification, and staff capacity. Any city-versus-Steward performance comparison must use legitimate precomputed data; no invented benchmarks.

The workforce path is staged: existing approved local businesses first; a workforce intermediary handling employment, insurance, training, and compliance second; a civic micro-work marketplace only later if workable. Low-risk cleanup and beautification are the potential scope, never electrical, roads, structures, utilities, or hazmat.

Future sensor thesis: **Flock detects. Steward resolves.** This concerns physical conditions rather than surveillance identities and depends on legitimate access and privacy governance. No integration this weekend.

Potential commercial layers are signal fusion, jurisdiction graph, policy engine, resolution network, trust/verification, funding/procurement, and a resolution graph linking problem, place, evidence, authority, provider, cost, time, rework, and durable outcome. Value would come from operating data, relationships, contracts, provider capacity, and distribution, not merely the open-source agent loop.

## Gates after Monday

1. Show the demo to an actual neighborhood operator. Ask: “Walk me through everything that makes this impossible in real life.”
2. Learn actual budgets, authorization, contracting, dispatch, verification, disputes, software, and staff pain.
3. Find one design partner and configure around its real workflow.
4. Resolve one real issue, even with substantial human coordination.
5. Build private modules only as demonstrated customer pain requires them.

Keep the public `steward` competition implementation under its released license. If the company proceeds, create a separate private `steward-platform` for new commercial work. Address legal/IP and contracting questions with appropriate counsel when relevant; they are not hackathon engineering tasks.
